Abstract

Real-time organism scenes become difficult to maintain when anatomy, motion, environmental effects, camera control and interface optics evolve as unrelated demonstrations. Pelagic is a browser-based rendering study that connects these systems while preserving an art-directed jellyfish identity. Its implementation combines a procedural mantle and folded oral membranes, pulse-coupled locomotion, persistent constrained appendages, bounded world-space currents, projected-size population detail, and an input-driven six-degree-of-freedom camera journey. A shared optical compositor samples one live ocean image for refractive bubbles, localized thermal shimmer and an interactive liquid clock. The clock combines authored implicit-surface choreography with a low-resolution persistent spring/advection field. This paper describes the actual Three.js 0.175.0 implementation, distinguishes code-equivalent equations from abstractions and artistic heuristics, and links the methods to reproducible source locations. The accompanying artifact includes browser motion evidence, deterministic tests, historical rejected approaches and fresh frame-interval measurements of the approved runtime. The work is a systems and visual-engineering study, not a fluid–structure interaction model, calibrated biological simulation or claim of algorithmic priority. Image-space optics cannot recover unseen radiance; liquid topology is not mass-conserving; performance and compatibility evidence remain specific to the tested hardware and backend.

Contributions

  1. Continuous procedural anatomy with pulse-coupled motion.
  2. Persistent constrained appendages across body motion and detail changes.
  3. Bounded currents connecting animals, wakes and layered marine snow.
  4. Projected-importance LOD with persistent resources and hysteresis.
  5. One live ocean source shared by several optical effects.
  6. Implicit clock topology coupled to persistent pointer deformation.

These are implementation contributions, not algorithmic-priority claims.

One-system architecture

Procedural animal

A continuous mantle and folded oral membranes retain their identity during contraction, refill and coast. This specimen uses the approved implementation, not a duplicate demonstration renderer. Unequal pulse windows couple bell motion to propulsion. These are art-directed kinematics, not measured biological energetics.

Full browser motion is available in the local review package. Published release assets have not been created.

Soft appendages

Persistent position history follows a moving root. Bounded forcing and length projection precede sheet/tube reconstruction. The oblique and underside views expose what a flattering frontal still can hide. The local geometry remains attached while distal membrane and tentacle history lags.

Locomotion and connected current

One activation brightens the animal and nearby particulate. Bounded world-space currents carry localized, finite-lifetime wakes downstream; a faint neighbor response extends the event. Neither the wake nor the activation is a global fluid solve or a permanent attached halo.

Population detail

Projected CSS-pixel diameter and hysteresis select anatomical detail. Medium thresholds are 32/24 pixels and near thresholds 110/90. Persistent resources and a 1.2-second adjacent-tier morph avoid repeated geometry creation. Camera travel changes importance while the approved organism state continues.

One world, several observers

Four authored views and Explore share one ocean. Input drives bounded scalar progress along baked camera positions and orientations. Camera mode changes are not new scenes. The publication does not redesign or correct the approved journey.

Live optics

Analytic entry and exit normals refract a view ray into a bounded lookup of the current ocean image. The moving background actually bends. This is not complete ray transport: transparent-layer depth, offscreen radiance and nested refraction are unavailable. The pinned WebGL depth-resolve limitation remains disclosed.

Abyssal sanctuary

Connected procedural basalt, processed scan detail, sparse ecology and a fixed-capacity plume establish a darker scale. Local eddies and thermal image distortion are visual approximations, not a temperature or buoyancy solve. Archived coral GLBs are not the active sanctuary geometry.

Ocean to glass

The clock forms over the same moving ocean. Implicit masses, authored contact terms and a small persistent spring/advection field produce the boundary and displacement. Two 256×180 RGBA16F field targets at the measured desktop viewport store state rather than a second ocean.

Contact and merge

Approach, contact and a narrowing neck are visible in ordinary-size browser motion. This is authored implicit topology with an optical response, not conserved liquid volume. Compare the complete source sequence with the contact filmstrip in the paper.

Deformation and recovery

Pointer input adds impulses to the persistent field; a damped spring/advection update retains and releases that deformation. The boundary and live-image lookup react together. Input scheduling and finite step admission are documented rather than called globally frame-rate independent.

Minute change

Changed clock cells retract and reconnect while unchanged cells retain their place. The review capture pins a local clock value to make the sequence repeatable. Its date is fixture input, not an asserted publication date.

The throat narrows and separates before the optical layer fades out. Ocean motion continues underneath. The local review provides the source frames and their timestamps, allowing the transition to be checked rather than judged only from a still.

Ablations and negative results

The paper distinguishes controlled diagnostic comparisons from historical rejected approaches. Rejected camera ownership, a giant analytic lens, bright repetitive geology, the separate idle renderer, malformed test input and the unapproved Asset2 AO experiment are documented in the supplement. They are not presented as a controlled superiority study.

Limitations

Artistic thrust, wakes and liquid topology are not calibrated fluid physics. Liquid volume is not conserved. Screen-space optics cannot recover offscreen radiance or exact transparent-layer depth. The current depth-resolve issue remains. Fixed steps do not make the whole application globally deterministic. This single-machine run does not validate hardware WebGPU, physical mobile, Safari or Firefox.
